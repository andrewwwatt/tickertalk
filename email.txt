🟢 MTH — Motus delivered one of its best results in years: HEPS 1,777c (+15%), dividend 710c (+29%), PBT R4.0bn (+20%). Net debt/EBITDA improved to 1.3x, ROIC of 12.6% exceeds WACC by 3%. At 6x PE, this automotive giant is priced like a cyclical at peak earnings — it's far more resilient than that.

🟢 APN — Aspen transformed its balance sheet with the R28bn APAC sale, ending net cash R0.8bn. Normalised HEPS +28% CER to 801.5c, Manufacturing EBITDA +21%, and a R2.0bn buyback. The reported loss is R2.3bn in once-off restructuring costs — ignore the noise, follow the R3.8bn in free cash flow.

🟡 WHL — Full-year numbers are solid but Q4 was brutal. Turnover R82.8bn (+4.2%), HEPS 282.3c (+5.3%), dividend 199c (+5.9%). Cash conversion surged to 104.5%. But H2 momentum slowed sharply and the consumer isn't getting stronger. Food remains the star; FBH is struggling. Oversold at RSI 28 but the earnings trajectory is flattening.

🟡 TFG — Africa doing the work (+3.4%), Australia drowning (-4.7%). Bash online sales surged 54.1%. But 85 stores closed with ~280 more projected. This is a business in restructuring mode, not growth mode. At 0.64x book it's pricing in the pain. Wait for Australia to stabilise.

🟡 THA — USD300m secured bond for Karo Platinum is a significant step — moving from equity-funded development to debt markets. At 4x PE and 0.53x book, Tharisa is cheap. But adding USD300m in leverage to a R4.5bn market cap company when PGMs are under pressure is bold. Watch the bond pricing.

🔴 CSB — Revenue grew 6% but HEPS fell 9% to 959.9c, operating profit down 15%. Final dividend slashed 22%. More stores closed than opened. Post-period trading flat. The building materials cycle has turned hostile and at 13.9x PE, Cashbuild isn't cheap enough to compensate. This dividend cut probably isn't the last.

🔴 SSW — United Steelworkers served strike notice at Stillwater East (~446 oz/day, 55% of US PGM output). CEO Richard Stewart's language is unprecedented: "if that plan cannot be implemented… there may ultimately be no viable basis for the continued operation." This isn't routine labour negotiation — it's existential for the US PGM operations.
