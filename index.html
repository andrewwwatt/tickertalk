Tongaat Hulett (TON)
Monthly report on the status of the business rescue proceedings
← Previous: NEUTRAL on 08 Oct
Tongaat Hulett issued its monthly business rescue status report. The sugar producer has been in business rescue proceedings since its financial collapse, triggered by debt levels that overwhelmed the business and a series of accounting irregularities. Monthly status reports are the BRPs' way of keeping the market informed while the rescue process continues. More than a year in, the question is whether a viable rescue plan exists or whether the process is heading toward liquidation.

Tongaat Hulett's monthly BRP updates have become a ritual — like a patient in long-term ICU. Every month the business rescue practitioners report that the process is continuing, conditions are being met, and creditors are being engaged. The sugar group's problems are structural: crushing margins, import competition from dumped global sugar, the legacy of the accounting scandal that triggered the collapse, and a balance sheet that should have been restructured years ago. The business rescue practitioners have been working through an incredibly complex set of issues — Tongaat owns milling assets across KZN, cane-growing supply arrangements, a property development portfolio (the most valuable part, arguably), and cross-border operations. Unwinding or restructuring that without destroying the underlying value has taken longer than anyone expected. The monthly updates don't give a timeline for conclusion, and there's no clarity on what the return to shareholders — if any — would look like. The stock has been suspended or delisted, and the BRP process is now in its extended phase. The most likely outcome is a debt-for-equity swap that wipes out existing shareholders. Rating: BEARISH. Business rescues that cross the one-year mark seldom end well for equity holders.

BEARISHReview #2
Newpark REIT (NRL)
Update on interim results publication date
← Previous: BULLISH on 10 Sep
Newpark REIT updated the market on the publication date of its interim results. NRL is a small-cap SA REIT — a portfolio of commercial and industrial properties. Delays or revisions to results publication dates in the REIT space are often a red flag. REITs are required to publish results within a fixed timetable (JSE Listings Requirements). Any variation from that timetable suggests either operational difficulty, valuation complexity (a forced write-down), or administrative delays — none of which is good news.

When a REIT updates the market on its results publication date, read it as a red flag until proven otherwise. SA REITs operate under strict JSE Listings Requirements — results must be published within four months of year-end (or three for interim). Any deviation from the expected calendar is unusual enough to warrant a separate SENS. Newpark REIT is a small-cap property play — industrial and commercial assets, not the raciest of sectors. Commercial property vacancy rates in SA remain elevated, industrial demand is mixed (transformation to logistics hubs is real but slow), and the interest rate cycle is still biting on floating-rate debt. A delay or push-out of the interim results date could mean the valuer needs more time on the portfolio, or — more concerning — the board and auditors are working through a significant impairment. At R4.70, NRL is thinly traded and institutionally unsponsored. A results-date update without the actual results is a holding pattern. We won't know the truth until the numbers land. Rating: BEARISH. Results-date updates in REITs are almost never benign.

BULLISHReview #1
Mondi plc (MNP)
Mondi Group Results of the Dividend Reinvestment Plan 2026 Interim Dividend
Mondi announced the results of its Dividend Reinvestment Plan for the 2026 interim dividend. A high scrip take-up means shareholders elected to receive shares instead of cash — a vote of confidence in the company's valuation and future. Mondi is a FTSE/JSE-listed packaging and paper group, the world's largest producer of corrugated packaging by some measures. When investors at a global packaging heavyweight choose scrip over cash, it's worth noting.

The Dividend Reinvestment Plan results are usually a quiet footnote, but the take-up ratio is a useful tell. When a high proportion of eligible shareholders take the scrip rather than the cash, it means they think the stock is undervalued and want to compound their position rather than take the dividend cheque. For Mondi — a FTSE 100 and JSE-listed packaging giant with operations across Europe, Russia (before the exit), and emerging markets — a strong scrip take-up is a signal from the shareholder base that the shares are cheap relative to the underlying business value. Mondi's packaging business (corrugated containers, containerboard, kraft paper) is cyclical but structurally essential. E-commerce continues to drive corrugated demand globally, and Mondi's integrated model (trees to paper to boxes) gives it a cost advantage over pure converters. The paper divestment programme (sale of the Russian assets, restructuring of the European fine-paper mills) has been reshaping the group into a pure-play packaging machine. At R172, the stock has been range-bound through 2026. A high DRP take-up suggests shareholders see the current price as an entry point. Rating: BULLISH. High scrip take-up is a quiet act of faith. When it happens at a global compounder, listen.

BEARISHReview #1
Grand Parade Investments (GPL)
Trading statement
Grand Parade Investments issued a trading statement, and the numbers don't look pretty. GPL is the investment holding company with exposure to SunWest (GrandWest Casino), food franchising (Burgers, Fish & Chips, etc.), and gaming technology. Trading statements from investment holding companies usually mean one or more of the underlying investee businesses is struggling. GPL has been a value trap for years — discount to NAV that never closes.

Grand Parade Investments is one of those JSE names that looks cheap on NAV but has been catching a falling knife for years. The trading statement tells you why: the discount to NAV exists because the assets themselves are under strain. SunWest Casino (GrandWest in Cape Town) faces the same headwinds as every SA casino — constrained consumer spending, load-shedding backup costs, and increasing online gambling competition from multi-nationals. The food franchising portfolio (Burgers & Fish, Fish & Chips, etc.) is exposed to the same stretched consumer. Gaming technology — the one bright spot — isn't big enough to offset the drag. At R1.69 the stock is more penny-stock than investment-grade. The trading statement is likely to confirm that earnings are under pressure. Investment holding companies work when the sum of the parts exceeds the whole. At GPL, the parts themselves are struggling. Rating: BEARISH. The discount to NAV is a warning, not an opportunity.

PDMR filings at CNP are rare, so one appearing warrants attention. The filing covers Persons Discharging Managerial Responsibilities — insiders beyond just the board. Without seeing the direction (buy or sell) and volume, this is a procedural disclosure, not a market signal.

Canal+ SA rarely lights up the SENS feed, so a PDMR filing is at least worth a glance. The company is effectively the JSE wrapper for MultiChoice Group, acquired by the French Canal+ group in a multi-step transaction that closed in 2023. The pay-TV business in Africa is under structural pressure: DStv premium subscribers are migrating to streaming (Showmax, Netflix, Amazon), advertising revenue is cyclical, and the sports rights inflation (Premier League, SuperSport) is relentless. Without knowing the direction, it's a watch-and-see. At R49.64, CNP is priced for the linear-TV decline. The Showmax pivot will determine if that's too pessimistic. Rating: NEUTRAL. PDMR filings at CNP are rare. Watch for pattern — one filing is noise, two in a quarter is signal.

Combined Motor Holdings (CMH)
Resignation of director and director appointment / board committee membership
Combined Motor Holdings announced both a director resignation and a new director appointment on the same day — a board refresh at KZN's largest motor group. CMH sells and services vehicles across KZN and the Eastern Cape, representing major OEMs like Toyota, Volkswagen, and Nissan. A director resignation plus simultaneous appointment suggests planned succession rather than a crisis departure. The new appointment to board committees signals where the board wants fresh expertise.

CMH had a busy Friday — one director out, one in, and board committees reshuffled. On its own, a director resignation SENS and an appointment SENS on the same day looks like a clean succession plan. One departs, another arrives, committees get reconfigured. That's good governance. The question is who left and why. A retirement at normal rotation is neutral. A departure with more time left on the expected term is a yellow flag. CMH is a well-run, regional motor group with high-quality OEM franchises (Toyota, VW, Nissan) and a strong service-and-parts aftermarket — margins are better in parts and service than in new-car sales. The motor retail environment in SA is tough: consumers are stretched, interest rates are biting, and new-car finance is expensive. CMH's KZN customer base is particularly exposed to the economic cycle. The board reshuffle matters because the right board composition is critical when the operating environment is deteriorating. At R37.89, CMH is a deep-value name trading well below its asset base — but value in motor retail has a long history of staying value. Rating: NEUTRAL. A planned succession is a non-event. The resignation's circumstances determine the signal.
