Oasis Crescent Property Fund (OAS)
Reviewed condensed consolidated interim results for the six-months ended 30 September 2026 and distribution declaration
Oasis Crescent's interim distribution fell 6.7% to 58.5c per unit as the Sacks Circle redevelopment removed income from its second-largest property. NAV per unit still rose 2.3% to R28.84 and the fund carries zero debt.

Oasis Crescent is a quality problem: a property fund with no debt, a low-risk tenant base (86% multinational, national or government-related) and growing NAV, but a distribution that is going backwards. The interim payout fell 6.7% to 58.5 cents per unit, and distributable income dropped 4.7% to R38.9 million. The single biggest reason is Sacks Circle — the fund's second-largest income generator — sitting dark while it's redeveloped into a world-class warehousing and logistics facility, due to be handed over around December. Losing income from your second-largest property will do that to a distribution.

Here's the thing though: this is a temporary, self-inflicted wound with a recovery date attached. The fund has zero leverage, NAV per unit rose 2.3% to R28.84, and vacancies are falling with little new supply in the market. At R29.40 the units trade at essentially book value with a fully-covered yield — that's the defensive profile this fund sells. But the distribution is the product, and a 6.7% cut stings income investors who bought the yield story. Hold for the Sacks Circle completion; the recovery is visible on the calendar. NEUTRAL.

BEARISHReview #2
Labat Africa (LAB)
Update on Hollard Insurance Company Limited Liquidation Application and Rescission Proceedings
← Previous: BEARISH on 27 Aug
The High Court has placed Labat Africa under provisional winding-up, dismissing its postponement and intervention bids. The company owes Hollard R20 million plus costs from a January 2024 judgment; a final winding-up hearing is set for 8 March 2027.

This is what the end of a JSE listing looks like. The Gauteng High Court has granted an order placing Labat Africa under provisional winding-up, dismissed its application for postponement, and dismissed its application to intervene. The trigger is a R20 million judgment in favour of Hollard Insurance dating back to January 2024, with costs on the attorney-and-client scale — the most punishing cost order available. The company's separate rescission proceedings remain technically alive, but the tone of this announcement suggests a board that has run out of road.

The timetable is grim for shareholders: the matter returns to court on 8 March 2027 to decide whether the winding-up becomes final, and virtually everyone — creditors, SARS, employees — has been ordered to be notified or served. The share price of R0.03 says the market has already written this one off. Labat was once a cannabis-and-healthcare story trading on dreams of what the plant could do; now it's a cautionary tale about what happens when the dream hits a R20 million judgment. There's no polite way to say it: this stock is a distressed asset, not an investment. BEARISH.

NEUTRALReview #1
Kumba Iron Ore (KIO)
Kumba production and sales report for the third quarter ended 30 September 2026
Kumba's Q3 production fell 19% to 7.5Mt as the R11.2bn UHDMS tie-in shut down Sishen's DMS plant, but sales rose 3% to 10.0Mt on stock drawdowns. Full-year guidance of 31-33Mt production and 35-37Mt sales is maintained.

Kumba's third quarter is a deliberate production sacrifice with full-year guidance intact — and the market has to decide which half of that sentence matters more. Production fell 19% to 7.5 million tonnes because the R11.2 billion UHDMS project at Sishen required the dense-media-separation plant to be shut down for the main tie-in. Sales, though, rose 3% to 10.0 million tonnes, funded by drawing down the 4.8 million tonnes of finished stock the company had deliberately banked. The full-year guidance of 31-33Mt production and 35-37Mt sales stands, and CEO Mpumi Zikalala is saying the right things about the premium the product still commands.

That premium deserves attention: Kumba realised US$86 per wet metric tonne year-to-date, a 9% premium over the benchmark, and Kolomela production was up 47% year-on-year. But the market backdrop is soft — weaker Chinese steel production, compressed mill margins and strong seaborne supply all weighed on prices through the quarter. The UHDMS project is the right long-term call: more premium-grade lump, less waste mining, extended mine life. But the 19% production drop is real, the stock at R224.31 is pricing in a lot of discipline, and iron ore's fortunes remain hostage to Beijing's building sites. On the numbers, this is a well-executed transition quarter with watchful eyes on the downside. NEUTRAL.

BULLISHReview #3
DRDGOLD (DRD)
Operating update for the quarter ended 30 September 2026
← Previous: BULLISH on 19 Aug
DRDGOLD's September quarter: gold production +10% to 42,021oz, revenue +31% to R2.95 billion and operating profit +42% to R1.63 billion, powered by a 24% higher dollar gold price. Costs are creeping (AISC +15%), but the gold price is doing the heavy lifting.

DRDGOLD is the gold-price trade in its purest JSE form, and the September quarter shows why. Gold produced rose 10% to 42,021 ounces, gold sold was up 14%, and revenue jumped 31% to R2.95 billion while operating profit surged 42% to R1.63 billion. The engine is the average gold price received: up 24% in dollars to US$4,247 an ounce. When the yellow metal runs, DRDGOLD's urban mining model — processing old mine dumps through Ergo and Far West Gold Recoveries — converts it straight into profit, with no exploration risk attached.

The caution flag is cost inflation. All-in sustaining costs rose 15% to US$2,169 per ounce, driven by higher diesel, reagents and electricity, plus the commissioning of the new elution plant and smelthouse at FWGR. Revenue growth still swamps cost growth — the margin between the gold price and AISC widened to over US$2,000 an ounce — but if gold retreats to US$3,800 while these costs stick, the leverage cuts the other way. At R42.48 the market is paying 8.7x earnings for one of the sector's highest-margin producers. In a bull market for gold, that's cheap. BULLISH.
