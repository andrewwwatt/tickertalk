Stadio Holdings (SDO)
Interim Results for the Period Ended 30 June 2026
H1 2026: Revenue R1.08bn (+13%), students 56,171 (+10%), Core HEPS 24.5c (+18%). Exceeded pre-listing 56k student milestone. New Durbanville campus opened with 1,250+ students.
Stadio has quietly become one of the most reliable compounders on the JSE. H1 revenue rose 13% to R1.08 billion, driven by 10% student growth to 56,171 — finally cracking the 56,000-student target set at listing. The new Durbanville campus opened ahead of expectations with over 1,250 students, and contact learning numbers surged 33% as the physical campus strategy gains traction. Core HEPS grew 18% to 24.5c, with the margin expansion coming from operating leverage as campuses fill up. The launch of engineering qualifications through the School of Engineering and Architecture opens a whole new growth vector. The 2030 ambition of 80,000 students and university status is looking less like a dream and more like a roadmap. The PE of 32.8 isn't cheap, but for a business growing earnings at 18% with negative beta, visible multi-year growth runway, and net cash on the balance sheet, the premium is earned. No interim dividend as capital is being reinvested into campus expansion — exactly what you want to see from a growth compounder.

Rainbow Chicken (RBO)
Group Financial Results for FY2026, Ordinary and Special Dividends
FY2026: Revenue R17.1bn (+7.7%), EBITDA R2.1bn (+102%), HEPS 150.87c (+130%), 135c total dividend incl. 75c special. Poultry pricing and feed costs both moving the right way.
Rainbow Chicken has gone from soggy to spectacular in twelve months. Revenue climbed 7.7% to R17.1 billion, but the real story is the margin expansion — EBITDA doubled to R2.1 billion as the EBITDA margin expanded from 6.7% to 12.5%. Firm chicken pricing, a strategic shift to higher-margin product mix, and lower feed costs all converged to deliver HEPS of 150.87 cents, up 130%. The board clearly agrees this is sustainable, declaring a final dividend of 45c PLUS a special dividend of 75c, bringing total distributions to 135c. With R2.4 billion in cash and debt/equity at just 0.05, this is a company drowning in cash and looking for ways to return it. The Waste-to-Value Rustenburg plant remains a work in progress, but the core Chicken Division is firing on all cylinders. At a PE of 6.3 and EV/EBITDA of 2.9, this is still priced like a commodity cyclical rather than the cash-compounding machine it's becoming. The special dividend signals management confidence in the earnings trajectory.

NEUTRAL Review #1
Globe Trade Centre (GTC)
Reviewed H1 2026 Results
H1 2026: Rental revenue €106m (+5%), gross margin €73m (+10%), FFO I €17m (+4%), loss after tax €18.1m. Net LTV 58.7% — deleveraging slow, occupancy stable at 87%.
GTC is doing the right things — just not fast enough to get excited about. Rental revenue grew a modest 5% to €106 million, with gross margin from rental activity up a more respectable 10% to €73 million. FFO I (the metric that matters for property companies) edged up 4% to €17 million, or €0.03 per share. But the bottom line still shows a loss after tax of €18.1 million, and EPRA NTA slipped 2% to €1.93 per share as property values remain under pressure in Central and Eastern Europe. The deleveraging story is the key catalyst — selling Avenue Mall in Zagreb, residential land in Budapest, and units in Germany — but net LTV actually crept UP to 58.7% from 57.0%. That's the wrong direction. Occupancy held at 87%, with Poland lagging. The Polish commercial property market needs rate cuts to re-rate, and until that happens, GTC remains a waiting game. Not expensive at 0.4x book, but cheap for a reason.

MILDLY BULLISH Review #1
Crookes Brothers (CKS)
Cautionary — Potential Disposal of Murrimo Macadamia
Selling Mozambique macadamia operations for nominal US$2.00 to extinguish ~US$5.4m in debt (R86m). Clean exit from a loss-making asset that's been dragging the group.
Crookes Brothers is doing the right thing by cutting loose its Mozambique macadamia albatross. The Murrimo operations are being sold for a nominal US$2.00, but the real prize is extinguishing US$5.4 million (R86 million) in debt — an 8-year term loan plus accrued interest that's been weighing on the balance sheet. This is a classic 'better to walk away' deal: the operations have been loss-making and the macadamia market remains under pressure, so offloading the debt liability is a net positive even at a token sale price. The shares rose 2.2% to R19.50, reflecting relief rather than euphoria. With ROE at -28.5% and interest cover barely above zero, Crookes needs more than this one deal to turn around. But at 0.36x book, any operational improvement can move the needle quickly. The Mozambique exit removes a major distraction and frees up management to focus on the core sugar and deciduous fruit operations. A small but meaningful step in the right direction.

ArcelorMittal SA (ACL)
Further Cautionary Announcement — Advanced Stage of Transaction
Negotiations with ArcelorMittal Group and IDC now at 'advanced stage'. The long-steel rescue deal is inching closer to finalisation but still no certainty.
AMSA's rescue saga continues, but the language is finally shifting from 'ongoing' to 'advanced stage'. The company confirmed that ArcelorMittal South Africa, the ArcelorMittal Group, and the IDC are now at an advanced stage to finalise the potential transaction — widely understood to be a rescue package for the loss-making long-steel operations at Newcastle and Vereeniging. The market popped the shares 5.6% to R1.32 on the day, but at a R1.9 billion market cap against R11.2 billion enterprise value, this remains a binary bet on a deal getting done. The balance sheet is distressed — negative equity, negative interest cover, current ratio at 0.58. Without a transaction, this company is in serious trouble. But an IDC-backed restructuring that offloads the long-steel mills could transform AMSA into a focused, profitable flat-steel business. The advanced-stage language is the most positive signal yet, but 'cautionary' is still in the headline. Deal risk remains very real, and the outcome is far from certain.
