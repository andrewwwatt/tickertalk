BEARISHReview #7
Sasol Limited (SOL)
Sasol tenders for its own convertible bonds — a sign of distress or discipline?
← Previous: BULLISH on 17 Nov
Sasol announced the results of its invitation to repurchase US$750 million of 4.5% guaranteed convertible bonds due 2027. The bond buyback reduces debt but the stock is getting hammered.

Sasol launched a tender offer for its US$750 million convertible bond pile, and the market reaction tells you everything — the stock dropped 5% on the day to R211.84, a far cry from the R300 it touched just a month ago. Buying back your own convertible bonds sounds prudent on paper: less debt, less dilution when bondholders convert. But the optics are terrible. Sasol is using cash to retire bonds at a time when its core chemicals business is under pressure from Chinese exports and a weak global economy.

The real question is whether this is smart liability management or a sign that management is worried about the conversion price being out of the money. Sasol's balance sheet has improved dramatically from the near-death experience of 2020, but the trajectory is going the wrong way — the stock is down 30% from its 52-week high, and buying bonds instead of investing in the business is not the signal shareholders want to see. Bearish because the market is right to be sceptical.

NEUTRALReview #1
Reinet Investments S.C.A. (RNI)
Half-year results — Richemont's shadow looms large
Reinet published consolidated unaudited interim results for the six months to September 2026. The investment holding company's fortunes remain tied to its massive Richemont stake.

Reinet is effectively a single-stock investment holding company with a tax domicile in Luxembourg and a chairman who happens to be Johann Rupert. Its half-year results are a reflection of Richemont's performance — when Cartier and Van Cleef sell diamonds to Chinese tourists, Reinet does well. When luxury demand softens, Reinet feels it. The numbers themselves are solid enough, but there is nothing in this statement that changes the investment case.

At R461.83 the stock trades at a discount to its net asset value, which is the standard Reinet pitch — you get Richemont exposure at a discount with a side bet on the private equity portfolio that has historically delivered mediocre returns. The discount has narrowed from the wide gap of 2025, but there is still value here if you believe luxury demand rebounds. Neutral because the results change nothing: Reinet is still Richemont in a holding company trench coat.

BULLISHReview #7
OUTsurance Group Limited (OUT)
Exchange ratio set for OHL minority squeeze-out — R91.78 per share
← Previous: NEUTRAL on 07 Oct
OUTsurance announced the exchange ratio for acquiring the remaining OHL minority shares. At R91.78 per share, it's a clean path to full ownership.

OUTsurance has set the exchange ratio for the minority squeeze-out of Outsurance Holdings (OHL) at R91.78 per share — a number that makes strategic sense even if it doesn't hand minority holders a fat premium. The group has been slowly consolidating ownership since the unbundling from Rand Merchant Investment Holdings, and this is the final step. At this price, OUTsurance is paying roughly 14 times earnings for the remaining 30% it doesn't own, which is reasonable for a business that generates the kind of recurring premium income OUT does.

What matters is what happens next. With full ownership, OUTsurance can stop managing to a minority-friendly dividend policy and start allocating capital the way the board wants — more into the Australian expansion, more into the direct insurance model that makes them the most efficient non-bank insurer in SA. The stock has run from R66 to R92 over the past year, and the multiple is still below where it deserves to be for a business compounding at 15% plus. Full ownership means full control, and that is worth something.

BULLISHReview #3
Attacq Limited (ATT)
TRP121 filing — someone is buying big at Attacq
← Previous: BULLISH on 16 Sep
Attacq filed a TRP121 notification of an acquisition of a beneficial interest in securities. When insiders or institutions accumulate, it pays to pay attention.

A TRP121 filing at Attacq means someone has crossed the 5% threshold — or an existing substantial holder has moved up. Attacq has been one of the more interesting property stories on the JSE: the Waterfall City development is a genuine success, the balance sheet is in decent shape, and the stock has been grinding higher from R15.65 to R17.80 over the past year. When someone files a TRP121, it means a sophisticated buyer sees more upside than the market is pricing in.

Attacq is the kind of property stock that institutional investors love when they bother to look — it owns the best node in Gauteng, has low vacancies, and the mall assets are actually performing. The risk is that Waterfall City is a single-bet property portfolio, and if that node slows down, there is no diversification to fall back on. But someone is buying, and that is usually a better signal than someone selling. Bullish because accumulation by informed money is the most reliable signal in small-cap property stocks.

BULLISHReview #2
Argent Industrial Limited (ART)
Interim results — steady as she goes, with a dividend to prove it
← Previous: BULLISH on 28 Sep
Argent Industrial reported interim results for the six months to September 2026 and declared a dividend. The steel fabricator keeps churning out cash in a sector that's supposed to be dead.

Argent Industrial keeps doing what it does: making steel products for mining, infrastructure and agriculture, paying a dividend, and ignoring the narrative that SA manufacturing is finished. The interim numbers for the six months to September show a business that understands its niche and doesn't try to be anything else. Revenue held up, margins stayed intact, and the dividend declaration is the signal that matters — management would not be paying one if the order book was shrinking.

The stock has recovered from a low of R27.60 to R46.00 and still trades on a single-digit earnings multiple. That is the market pricing in the risk that every SA industrial carries — load shedding may be gone but logistics, municipal dysfunction and input cost inflation are not. Argent is not going to double overnight, but it is going to keep printing cash, and at this price you are getting paid a dividend to wait.
