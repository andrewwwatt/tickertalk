We Buy Cars Holdings Limited (WBC)
Audited results for year ended 30 September 2026 — HEPS -18.6%, founders step back
Revenue up 7.2% to R28.3bn, core headline earnings -18.5% to R764.2m, HEPS -18.6% to 182.8c. Used-car price deflation and competition from cheap Asian new cars squeezed margins.

We Buy Cars sold more cars and made less money. Revenue grew 7.2% to R28.3 billion and the group shifted a record 186,220 vehicles, but core headline earnings fell 18.5% to R764 million and headline earnings per share dropped 18.6% to 182.8 cents. The culprit is not operational — the buying and selling machine works fine — it is structural: cheap Asian new cars are crowding the entry-level market, pushing used-car prices down and squeezing margins that were already thin. Management described it as a 'deflationary used-vehicle market' and the response was to cut selling prices to keep inventory turning. The right call commercially, but one that trashed profitability. A final dividend of just 17 cents, down 43%, confirms the board knows the game has changed.

The big strategic news is that co-founders Faan and Dirk van der Walt are stepping back from executive roles. Wynand Beukes takes over as CEO. The founders built this business from nothing into a R28 billion revenue machine over two decades, and leadership transitions at founder-led companies are rarely smooth. The cash generation is excellent — R1 billion in operating cash flow, up 48% — and the 131% cash conversion ratio shows the business model still works. But the market has repriced this stock from R54 to R25 for a reason, and a 27% dividend payout ratio does not suggest management is confident about the near term. BEARISH because margin compression in a deflationary market is not a one-quarter problem, it is a new reality, and the new CEO has not yet proven he can navigate it.

Shoprite Holdings Limited (SHP)
Shoprite Financial Services enters strategic partnerships with DNI and Investec
← Previous: BULLISH on 19 Nov
Shoprite Financial Services partners with DNI and Investec (19.99% stake) to create an alliance banking offering targeting Shoprite's massive customer base.

Shoprite has been quietly building the pieces for a serious financial services play for years, and this announcement puts all the pieces on the board. The group is partnering with DNI, the fintech that has been powering its Money Market counters, and bringing in Investec as a 19.99% shareholder in Shoprite Financial Services with a mandate to provide banking infrastructure and balance sheet support. The result is an alliance banking model that gives Shoprite customers access to transactional accounts, debit cards, and credit products without Shoprite having to carry the capital requirements of a full banking licence. It is the same model that made Capitec a R200 billion company: take a simple banking product, distribute it through high-footfall retail locations, and price it aggressively.

Shoprite has 2,000+ stores, a loyalty programme with millions of active users, and a brand that lower-income South Africans trust more than they trust any bank. If the execution is even half-decent, this partnership produces a financial services business worth multiples of what the market is currently assigning to Shoprite's 'other income' line. The risk is execution — DNI is unproven at scale, and the Investec partnership creates complexity that a simple retailer is not built to manage — but the upside is so large that the stock should be pricing in more than the 1.9% it got on the day. BULLISH because the combination of Shoprite's distribution, Investec's balance sheet and DNI's tech is the strongest challenger to the incumbent banks since Capitec launched its savings account.

Reunert Limited (RLO)
Consolidated financial statements for year ended 30 September 2026 — HEPS -48%
← Previous: NEUTRAL on 13 Nov
Revenue down 2% to R13.55bn, operating profit -44% to R852m, HEPS -48% to 339c. Copper prices up 33%, strong rand, delayed defence orders all hit. Dividend flat at 383c total.

Reunert's 2026 financial year was a horror show of external factors that the company could not control and one or two it probably should have. Revenue fell 2% to R13.55 billion, which is manageable, but operating profit collapsed 44% to R852 million and headline earnings per share halved to 339 cents from 649 cents. Copper prices rose 33%, crushing margins at African Cables and sending customers to the sidelines. The rand strengthened 8.4% against the dollar and 3.7% against the euro, hammering export businesses. And the Middle East conflict delayed defence orders that would have landed before year-end. That is a triple-whammy that no diversified industrial could have fully hedged, and Reunert did not come close.

There was a R67 million goodwill write-off at Omnigo and Reutech Communications, R43 million in higher expected credit losses (mostly municipalities and SOEs that simply stopped paying), and the Lumika solar joint venture contributed a R33 million loss. The defence order book actually closed 25% higher than last year, with R3 billion of contracted and negotiated work ahead — so 2027 should be better — but the market is not wrong to punish this. At R47.50 the stock trades on 14 times depressed earnings, and management held the dividend flat at 383 cents, which is a signal of confidence if not exactly generosity. BEARISH because the recovery is a story of hope, not execution, and 2026 was a year the company would rather forget.

The risk is always regulatory (National Health Insurance lingers as a theoretical threat) and the new CEO brings execution uncertainty. But the numbers here are clean, the cash generation is real, and the market is not paying for any of the upside. BULLISH because this is a quality business the market has decided to be grumpy about, and grumpy markets create opportunity.

Deneb Investments Limited (DNB)
Category 1 disposal — Formex sold for R435m enterprise value
Deneb is selling its wholly owned automotive component manufacturer Formex Industries to VM Automotive for an enterprise value of R435 million. A R40 million loan claim is retained and will be repaid within 30 days of closing.

Deneb is selling Formex, its automotive components subsidiary, to VM Automotive for an enterprise value of R435 million. This is a Category 1 disposal — the largest transaction a company can do without shareholder approval being automatic. The purchase consideration is based on an agreed enterprise value of R435 million on a cash-free, debt-free basis, and critically, Deneb is retaining a R40 million loan claim that Formex must repay within 30 days of closing. That R40 million is effectively a cash bonus that arrives shortly after the deal closes.

The logic of the disposal is straightforward: Deneb is exiting a capital-intensive manufacturing business in a sector facing structural headwinds. At R2.85, Deneb's market cap is roughly R300 million, meaning the Formex sale alone justifies most of the company's current valuation. The remaining portfolio — property and other investments — is essentially free. That is a compelling setup for shareholders who trust management to deploy the cash wisely. The risk is that they do not, but the structure of this deal suggests discipline, not desperation.

Astral Foods Limited (ARL)
Audited results for year ended 30 September 2026 — HEPS +102%, total dividend +102%
← Previous: BULLISH on 29 Oct
Revenue up 3% to R23.3bn, operating profit +88% to R2.35bn, HEPS +102% to 4,439c from 2,193c. Total dividend doubled to 2,220c. Feed costs drove the recovery as maize prices fell 23%.

Astral Foods cooked up a beauty of a recovery year. Revenue grew a modest 3% to R23.3 billion, but operating profit nearly doubled — up 88% to R2.35 billion — and headline earnings per share more than doubled to 4,439 cents from 2,193 cents. The story is brutally simple: maize and soya prices collapsed. SAFEX yellow maize averaged R3,485 a tonne versus R4,552 last year, a 23% drop, and the Poultry Division — which accounts for 83% of revenue — saw its operating profit surge 188% to R1.53 billion because chicken feed got much cheaper and chicken prices stayed firm. The Feed Division actually saw profit fall 14% because its selling prices followed raw material costs down, but that is a rounding error in a group that went from a 5.5% operating margin to 10.1%.

Management doubled the total dividend to 2,220 cents a share, closed the year with R1.4 billion in cash, and net asset value per share rose 17.5% to R163.91. The risks are real — El Niño threatens the next maize crop, the US/Iran war has pushed fuel costs higher and squeezed consumer wallets, and the prior year's avian influenza losses were always going to be a low base for comparison. But a poultry producer with this kind of balance sheet, paying a 12.5% dividend yield at R177, is pricing in a margin collapse that has not happened. BULLISH because cheap maize and efficient chicken production is a combination that prints money, and Astral is the best cost producer in the room.
