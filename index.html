BULLISHReview #1
Vukile Property Fund Limited (VKE)
Strategic acquisition of SA retail properties from Old Mutual + accelerated bookbuild
Vukile is buying Old Mutual's SA retail property portfolio and launching an accelerated bookbuild to fund it. A bold, well-timed acquisition from SA's best retail landlord.

This is exactly the kind of deal Vukile should be doing. SA retail property has been through the wringer — weak consumer spending, high interest rates, loadshedding — but the cycle is turning. Inflation is moderating, the SARB has room to cut, and the consumer is showing signs of life. Buying a portfolio of quality retail assets from a motivated seller (Old Mutual does not want to be a landlord) at what is likely a distressed valuation is textbook REIT value creation.

The accelerated bookbuild means Vukile is raising equity to fund the deal, which will dilute existing holders in the short term. But the quality of the assets and the strategic fit — Vukile already runs SA's best retail portfolio — mean the long-term accretion is real. At R25.35, Vukile yields around 8.5% and trades at a discount to NAV. This deal could be the catalyst that closes that gap.

BULLISHReview #3
Telkom SA SOC Ltd (TKG)
Cautionary: Telkom confirms discussions with MTN — the prize is a premium
← Previous: BEARISH on 01 Oct
Telkom explicitly confirmed it is in discussions with MTN. After years of speculation, Telkom holders finally have a potential exit at a premium.

Telkom's cautionary is the clearest signal yet that the company is open to a deal. The board has spent years trying to prove Telkom could be a standalone mobile contender — building a network, cutting costs, defending the enterprise base. It has not worked. The mobile business has roughly 8 million subscribers against MTN's 35 million and Vodacom's 45 million, and the fixed-line business generates cash that gets eaten by legacy costs. A sale to MTN at a control premium is the best outcome available.

For shareholders, this is a lottery ticket with decent odds. Telkom's current price of R69 capitalises the group at roughly R36 billion — cheap for the spectrum and infrastructure it owns. A bid at R90-100 would be transformative. The risk is regulatory: the Competition Commission and Icasa will have views on MTN owning Telkom's spectrum and fixed assets. But the horse is out of the stable. Telkom holders should sit tight and wait for the number.

← Previous: NEUTRAL on 13 Nov
SPAR disclosed that a shareholder has requested a general meeting. The wholesale distributor has been through a rough patch and activist shareholders are circling.

NEUTRALReview #1
Sephaku Holdings Limited (SEP)
Interim results for six months ended 30 September 2026
Sephaku released interim results (after an earlier cancellation and re-filing). The cement and building materials business is grinding through a tough construction cycle.

Sephaku is a small cap in a tough industry. SA cement demand is depressed by low fixed investment, weak municipal infrastructure spending, and a construction sector that has been in recession for years. Sephaku's Aganang cement plant is one of the newest and most efficient in the country, but efficiency means nothing when there are not enough customers to buy the product. The interim results will show whether Sephaku is holding market share and managing costs.

The cancellation and re-filing of the results announcement is a red flag — administrative errors do not inspire confidence. At R2.05, the stock is cheap on a price-to-book basis, but cheap for a reason. Cement is a cyclical business that needs a construction cycle to turn. Until government infrastructure spending materialises and the private sector starts building again, Sephaku will remain a value trap. Watch for signs of volume recovery. Until then, this is a wait-and-see.

BULLISHReview #4
Old Mutual Limited (OMU)
Disposal of SA policyholder retail property portfolio to Vukile — cleaning house
← Previous: NEUTRAL on 17 Sep
Old Mutual is selling its SA policyholder retail property portfolio to Vukile Property Fund. Another step in simplifying the Old Mutual behemoth.

Old Mutual keeps shrinking the parts of the business that should never have been there. A life insurer should not own shopping centres — it should own financial assets that match its liability profile. The policyholder retail property portfolio is exactly the kind of legacy asset that made Old Mutual a conglomerate discount case for decades. Selling it to Vukile, a pure-play retail REIT, puts the assets in hands that know how to manage them and frees Old Mutual's balance sheet for the business it actually understands.

Vukile, for its part, gets a portfolio of 'landmark' retail properties at a time when SA retail is recovering from the worst of the cost-of-living crisis. The accelerated bookbuild to part-fund the deal suggests Vukile is leaning into the acquisition aggressively. Old Mutual at R14.84 is still cheap for a diversified financial services group with an asset management business, a bank, and a restructured SA life operation. Every divestment makes the sum-of-parts clearer.

Loss-of-life incident at Mponeng mine
← Previous: BULLISH on 10 Nov
Harmony reported a fatal incident at its Mponeng mine. A sobering reminder of the human cost of mining, even at one of the world's best-run gold operations.

A loss-of-life incident at Mponeng is always a gut punch. Mponeng is Harmony's flagship — the deepest mine in the world, a marvel of engineering, and the cornerstone of the group's production profile. The safety systems at Mponeng are best-in-class, which makes any fatality there particularly jarring. The mine will be shut down for investigation, production will be affected, and the Section 54 stoppage is almost certain.

A temporary shutdown will hit quarterly production but the long-term thesis — Harmony is a low-cost, high-grade gold producer in a gold bull market — is unchanged. The real cost is human. Every mining fatality is one too many, and the industry knows it. The stock will dip on the news, but gold bugs should use it as a buying opportunity.

Copper 360 Limited (CPR)
Rietberg Mine reaches main ore zone — bulk sampling supports mine plan grade
← Previous: NEUTRAL on 28 Sep
Copper 360 announced that its Rietberg Mine development has reached the main ore zone, with initial bulk sampling supporting the mine plan grade. A big milestone for a small cap with big ambitions.

Copper 360 is the kind of high-risk, high-reward story that SA small-cap mining investors love. The company is building a copper mine in the Northern Cape, and reaching the main ore zone is a genuine operational milestone. Bulk sampling supporting the mine plan grade means the geological model is holding up — the ore is where they thought it was, and at the grade they expected. For a development-stage miner, that is the single biggest risk to retire.

The stock trades at R0.67, so this is not for widows and orphans. Copper prices are elevated on the back of electrification demand and constrained supply, which gives CPR a strong macro tailwind if they can get the mine into production without blowing the budget. The next catalyst is the feasibility study and production timeline. If they deliver on schedule, this stock goes much higher. If they don't, the market will punish them ruthlessly. High risk, high reward.
