BULLISHReview #1
Tiger Brands Limited (TBS)
Audited FY results — Revenue +3.3%, final dividend lifted
Revenue up 3.3%, volumes up 4.5% — underlying operational momentum despite a tough consumer environment. Final dividend increased, signalling confidence in cash generation.

Tiger Brands served up a decent set of full-year numbers. Strip out the one-offs, and you get revenue up 3.3% with volumes growing 4.5%, which in this economy is a statement. The capital investment plan is delivering, margin expansion is real, and management felt confident enough to lift the final dividend. That is not nothing when consumers are squeezing every rand.

The stock reacted accordingly — jumped 11% on results day before settling. The question is whether Tiger can keep the momentum going through FY2027. Input costs, consumer strain, and competitive pressure in staples are all headwinds. But for now, this is a business executing well in a tough market. The dividend hike is the tell: management sees the cash flows to back it up.

BULLISHReview #2
Life Healthcare Group Holdings Limited (LHC)
FY results — NEPS +9.5%, dividend +14.3%, muted revenue
← Previous: BULLISH on 13 Nov
Normalised EPS up 9.5% to 109.8c, revenue +1.7% to R25.5bn, final dividend up 14.3% to 40c. Margin expansion of 80bps on cost discipline. Share buyback underway.

Life Healthcare delivered a textbook operational recovery. Revenue was barely up at 1.7%, but normalised EBITDA jumped 6.7% with 80bps of margin expansion, and normalised EPS grew 9.5%. That is cost discipline paying off. The 14.3% dividend increase and the R11.75 average buyback price tell you management thinks the stock is undervalued.

The headline earnings comparisons are messy — last year had the R5.3bn LMI disposal gain and a R2.9bn Piramal fair value loss, so reported EPS fell 65%. Ignore that noise. The underlying business is running well: 158 new beds coming online including Paarl Valley Hospital, the R400m cost programme is on track, and ROCE sits at a healthy 17.7%. At 1.06x net debt/EBITDA, the balance sheet has firepower. This is a steady compounder trading at a reasonable multiple.

iOCO Limited (IOC)
Acquisition of Intellection — vertical market software play
← Previous: NEUTRAL on 13 Oct
iOCO acquired 100% of Intellection, a SA vertical market software specialist in financial supply chain automation. Price undisclosed, earn-out structure attached.

iOCO keeps buying its way to a turnaround. Intellection is a founder-led software business that helps corporate clients automate financial supply chains on a global platform — sounds like a sensible tuck-in for iOCO's vertical market software portfolio. The price is undisclosed (always a yellow flag), with an earn-out tied to growth targets over two years.

The strategy is clear: bolt on high-quality niche software businesses, give them access to iOCO's platform and capital, let them run autonomously. It worked for the MySky acquisition earlier this year. But the sum of these parts needs to eventually show in the group numbers. FY2026 results were encouraging — profit after tax up 46% — and the acquisition pipeline is buoyant. NEUTRAL because the deal is small and undisclosed, but the trajectory is improving.
