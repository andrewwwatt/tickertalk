BEARISHReview #3
Mantengu (MTU)
Disposal of Sublime Technologies Proprietary Limited
← Previous: BEARISH on 30 Sep
Mantengu is selling its silicon carbide subsidiary Sublime Technologies for exactly R1,001 — a symbolic disposal that confirms the Eskom tariff negotiation failure killed the business. Sublime has generated no income since June 2025. The stock trades at R0.18 with a market cap of R58.5m against R775m in enterprise value.

A disposal for R1,001. That's not a typo — one thousand and one rand for a business that once produced silicon carbide in Kriel, Mpumalanga. Mantengu's board has accepted that Sublime Technologies is effectively worthless after Eskom refused to approve a new electricity tariff agreement. The company stopped generating any income in June 2025 and has been burning cash ever since. The buyer is a consortium of family trusts represented by Dr Timothy Tebeila, which at least means the disposal is arms-length. But the symbolism is brutal: a business that couldn't get a competitive electricity tariff in a country where energy costs make or break industrial processes.

Mantengu itself trades at R0.18 with a market cap of just R58.5m — and an enterprise value of R775m, meaning there's over R700m in debt sitting on a micro-cap balance sheet. The Altman Z-Score of -0.46 and Piotroski F-Score of 1 (out of 9) are flashing distress signals. Shares outstanding have ballooned 50% in one year, and ROE is -85.2%. Cleaning house by selling dead assets is the right thing to do, but it doesn't change the fundamental math: this is a company with negative equity value on an enterprise basis. BEARISH — the disposal is a necessary step, not a turnaround signal.

Dis-Chem Pharmacies (DCP)
Interim Consolidated Financial Results for the six months ended 31 August 2026 and Dividend Declaration
← Previous: NEUTRAL on 15 Sep
Dis-Chem grew revenue 8% to R23bn, driven by the Better Rewards loyalty programme, but EPS fell 13.3% to 64.1c and the dividend was cut 13% to 25.62c per share. The loyalty programme is winning customers and compressing margin at the same time — a strategic trade-off that will take another year to play out.

The headline numbers tell a story of a retailer winning the volume game but losing on margin. Revenue hit R23bn, up 8%, and Dis-Chem points to the Better Rewards programme as the main driver. One year in, the programme has returned R1.5bn in instant value to customers — that's real loyalty currency, and it shows in foot traffic. But 45% of front shop retail sales experienced temporary price deflation, and the cost of rewarding customers shows up in the P&L: gross profit margin compressed, EPS dropped 13.3% to 64.1c, and the interim dividend was trimmed to 25.62c per share. The market barely reacted — the stock closed flat at R27.95.

The question is whether Better Rewards becomes a moat or a margin-destroying arms race. Management argues that once the base effects from the programme's first anniversary unwind, natural inflation will support retail revenue growth. They're probably right, but the 24.5x trailing PE already prices in a lot of optimism. At 4.2x book with an 18.96% ROE, this isn't a broken business — it's a good business going through a margin reset. The next six months will tell us whether the programme economics work. NEUTRAL — watch the margin trajectory.

If you believe grain prices stay soft and the consumer doesn't completely collapse, this is priced like a distressed business delivering record earnings. The final results drop on 23 November. BULLISH.

Another quarter, another predictable beat from the world's largest brewer. AB InBev grew Q3 revenue 4.9% (9.1% reported, helped by FX) to US$16.5bn, normalized EBITDA ticked up 3.9% to US$5.96bn, and underlying profit rose 21.6% to US$2.4bn. Revenue per hectolitre grew 4.2%, the megabrands — led by Corona at +20% outside its home market — continue to gain share, and no-alcohol beer revenue jumped 35%. The company also reported a 45% surge in GMV from its digital marketplace. This is a well-oiled global beverage machine printing US$6.7bn in underlying profit through nine months.

So why is the JSE-listed ANH down 7% at R1,302? The margin contraction is the culprit — normalized EBITDA margin shrank 34bps to 36.1% on higher input costs and currency headwinds. In a market obsessed with margin expansion, a 34bp contraction is enough to trigger a rotation out of staples. For a JSE investor, the secondary listing adds FX complexity and liquidity risk. On 7.1x trailing PE with a global portfolio of irreplaceable brands, this isn't expensive — but it's also not the most exciting thing you can own in SA today. NEUTRAL.

Staffing Solutions SA delivered revenue and gross profit growth, Professional Services Australia improved profitability, and the net unrestricted cash position sits at R174.2m. The stock barely moved on the day (+0.8%), which tells you nobody read past the revenue line. BULLISH — this is value territory with a dividend that's actually growing.
