Pepkor's credit rating review by Moody's

NEUTRALReview #3
Orion Minerals (ORN)
Board Succession and Appointment of New Chair
← Previous: NEUTRAL on 21 Oct
Orion transitions board leadership as it moves from explorer to developer. Prieska and Okiep copper assets are real, but capital remains the missing piece.

**Orion Minerals** is changing the driver while the car is still moving. A board succession with a new chair signals the transition from founder-led explorer to development-stage operator. The assets — Prieska Copper-Zinc and Okiep Copper Project in the Northern Cape — are among the most advanced base-metal development stories on the JSE. But they remain pre-production, pre-cashflow, and dependent on raising development capital.

The copper thesis is strong (the world needs more copper, SA has world-class deposits), but patience has limits. A board refresh at this stage can go two ways: fresh capital-market relationships and operational discipline, or founder fatigue signalling the easy part is done. The market will know which by whether the new chair brings a funding pathway. Watch for the next capital-raising update.

BULLISHReview #2
Oando PLC (OAO)
9M 2026 Unaudited Results Release
← Previous: BULLISH on 04 Aug
Oando swung from an operating loss of N109.7bn to a profit of N159.7bn on 14% higher production, 22% lower unit costs, and debt down 22% from its peak. The balance sheet reset is working.

**Oando** has been the JSE's most interesting Nigerian energy story, and these 9M numbers finally show the operational turnaround matching the narrative. Production hit 42,985 boepd (up 14%), facility uptime jumped from 85% to 96.1%, and unit opex fell 22% to $15.39/boe. Revenue rose 9% to N2.78 trillion and operating profit swung from a N109.7bn loss to N159.7bn profit. That's not just a recovery — it's a different business.

The headline PAT of N106.5bn is down 47%, but that's because 9M 2025 had a non-recurring N321bn finance income item. Strip it out and operating leverage is finally flowing through. Total borrowings are down 22% from the FY2024 peak to N2.16trn, with N285bn in cash generated from operations. The N500bn rights issue (SEC-pending) and US$300m debt-to-equity conversion are the last big moves. If they land this, Oando becomes one of the continent's most credible energy stories. At current Nigerian share prices, the JSE listing is the cheap way in.

BULLISHReview #1
Nu-World Holdings (NWL)
Reviewed Condensed Annual Results — Year Ended 31 August 2026
Nu-World's revenue slipped 1.8% but HEPS ticked up 0.9% to 395.5c and the dividend jumped 24.8% to 185.30c. Flat earnings and a dividend hike is a win in this consumer environment.

**Nu-World** imports, assembles and distributes branded consumer goods across SA and internationally. The headline looks soft — revenue down 1.8% to R2.25bn — but dig deeper and there's real resilience. Basic EPS slipped just 0.1% to 391.3c, while HEPS eked out a 0.9% gain to 395.5c. In a year when SA consumers are under more pressure than at any point since the load-shedding nightmare, that's a solid result.

What stands out is the dividend: up 24.8% to 185.30c per share. That's a statement. Nu-World could have conserved cash but chose to reward shareholders meaningfully. The group's offshore diversification (Australia, Brazil, UAE, Hong Kong) helps smooth the SA consumer cycle — revenue was down just 1.8% despite brutal local conditions. With a PE of 6.27, P/B of 0.37, and ROE of 5.53%, it's no growth rocket, but it's a steady income payer in a sea of retail destruction. That counts for something in this market.

BEARISHReview #3
Finbond Group (FGL)
Category 2 Announcement — Acquisition of Rigel Park
← Previous: BEARISH on 23 Oct
Finbond buys Rigel Park for R55.5m cash — right after interim results showed profit before tax crashing 79.7%. Buying property while earnings are in freefall is a choice.

**Finbond** is acquiring Rigel Park in Pretoria for R55.5m cash — the very business park where it already leases space. The rationale (own your premises like a proper bank) is sound. The seller is Zungu Investments (Sandile Zungu's vehicle). Category 2 means no shareholder vote needed. On paper, it's a clean property deal.

The problem is context. Ghost Mail recently covered Finbond's interim results: profit before tax crashed 79.7%, headline loss per share, and net interest income grew just 1.7% despite loans and advances rising 14.5%. Credit impairments are surging as SA consumers buckle under unsecured debt. And now Finbond wants to spend R55.5m cash on real estate? The stock trades at 84 cents, down 26% in a year. Property ownership is a nice-to-have when your core business generates returns. Right now, Finbond's core business is generating losses. This reads like rearranging deck furniture.
