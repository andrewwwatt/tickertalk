Transnet SOC Limited (ZZZ)
Audited Annual Financial Statements for the Year Ended 31 March 2026 with Unmodified Audit Opinion
Transnet swung to a R4.6bn profit from a R1.9bn loss — its first profit in four years. Revenue up 7.1% to R88.6bn. Rail volumes up to 167.9m tonnes from 160.1m. Unmodified audit opinion. The reforms under CEO Michelle Phillips and Chair Andile Sangqu are starting to work. A genuinely positive signal for SA Inc.

Transnet just posted its first profit in four years — R4.6bn from a R1.9bn loss — and received an unmodified audit opinion, which for a state-owned enterprise that has been through governance chaos is almost as significant as the profit itself. Revenue grew 7.1% to R88.6bn, rail volumes increased to 167.9m tonnes, and petroleum volumes rose to 14.2bn litres. Container throughput slipped slightly — the Durban and Cape Town port issues haven't been fully resolved — but the direction of travel is unmistakably positive. CEO Michelle Phillips has reinforced execution and integration across divisions, and chair Andile Sangqu is cautiously optimistic about the path ahead. The R70bn investment plan and private sector participation programme are the next catalysts. The balance sheet is still under pressure from legacy debt, but the operating turnaround is real. This matters for every SA Inc. investor — Transnet's logistics performance directly impacts mining exports, retail supply chains, and manufacturing competitiveness. Rating: BULLISH. Not a listed investment (yet — privatisation chatter persists), but the turnaround is the most important economic signal in SA right now.
