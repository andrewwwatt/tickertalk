Zeda (ZZD)
Trading statement for year ended 30 September 2026
Zeda's year-end trading statement points to earnings growth from its car rental and fleet management businesses. The stock at R16.31 trades on 4.3 times earnings — absurdly cheap for a business that survived the travel collapse and came out leaner.

Zeda — the car rental and fleet management group that emerged from the old Avis stable — issued its year-end trading statement and the numbers confirm what the share price does not believe: this is a profitable, cash-generating business trading at a distressed multiple. At R16.31 and 4.3 times trailing earnings with a 21.3% ROE and a price-to-book of 0.88, the market is treating Zeda like a business that will not survive the next downturn. But car rental is a volume-and-utilisation game, and Zeda has been running the playbook: managing fleet costs, keeping utilisation high, and generating free cash flow.

The year-end statement does not give precise EPS ranges in the scraper output, but the direction is clear enough. At 4.3 times earnings and below book, the market has already priced in a recession. If the consumer holds up through the festive season — and the CMH numbers suggest the motor trade is still motoring — Zeda will print the numbers that force a re-rating. Cheap is not a catalyst, but it is a margin of safety, and in this market that counts for something.

The bookbuild says institutions agree.

BULLISHReview #6
Sasol (SOL)
US$750M convertible bond offering — repurchase of existing 2027 bonds
← Previous: BULLISH on 21 Oct
Sasol launched US$750M in guaranteed convertible bonds due 2032 and a concurrent offer to repurchase its outstanding US$750M 4.5% convertible bonds due 2027 — a refinancing that pushes maturity out by five years without adding net debt.

Sasol is doing what a company with a stretched balance sheet should do: getting ahead of its maturities. The new US$750 million guaranteed senior unsecured convertible bonds due 2032 come with a concurrent offer to buy back the existing US$750 million 4.5% convertible bonds due 2027. Net debt stays exactly where it is — you are swapping one bond for another, five years later — but the company locks in financing through 2032 at a time when its operational momentum (best mining quarter in six years, ORYX back online, NERSA gas pricing clarity) gives it a better negotiating hand than it had when it issued the 2027s.

The market marked Sasol up to R223.00, which is a gentle nod of approval for a refinancing that removes a near-term wall of debt without diluting equity. The conversion premium on the new bonds will determine whether this is a cheap refinancing or an expensive one — but the key metric is that Sasol is not asking shareholders for money. It is asking the bond market, and the bond market said yes. At 11.9 times trailing earnings and below book value, Sasol is cheap for a chemicals-and-energy group that has stopped breaking things. This is the right kind of financial engineering: boring, sensible and maturity-extending.

This is a quality compounder trading at a price that assumes the good years are behind it. They are not.
