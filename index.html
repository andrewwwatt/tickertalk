Sanlam Limited (SLM)
9-month update — new business +20% but earnings flat
← Previous: BULLISH on 04 Nov
New business volumes grew 20% and net client cash flows surged 37% to R108 billion. But core earnings only rose 1% and value of new covered business fell 13%, reflecting margin pressure.

Sanlam's 9-month update is the definition of mixed. The top-line metrics are excellent — new business volumes up 20%, net client cash flows up 37% to R108 billion — but the bottom line tells a different story. Core earnings inched up just 1%, operating profit excluding investment variances fell 1%, and the value of new covered business dropped 13% to R1.5 billion with margins compressed to 1.7%. The message is that Sanlam is selling more but earning less on each sale, which is the classic insurance industry squeeze.

The discretionary capital balance of R1.9 billion and economic solvency cover within target range provide comfort, but the market wants to see earnings growth, not just asset gathering. At R76.53 the stock is down 9.6% in a month and the market is questioning the earnings trajectory. Neutral because the asset gathering story is strong but the earnings conversion is not there yet.

BULLISHReview #4
Shoprite Holdings Limited (SHP)
Collapse of the non-economic voting structure — goodbye Wiese era
← Previous: BULLISH on 09 Nov
Shoprite announced a proposed transaction to collapse the existing non-economic voting structure linked to Christo Wiese's Titan/Thibault entities, simplifying the share structure and removing a governance overhang.

This is the biggest news on the JSE today, and it is not close. Shoprite has been carrying a bizarre dual-class voting structure since the Wiese era — deferred shares that gave Titan/Thibault outsized voting power without economic exposure. That structure has been a governance stain for years, and Shoprite is finally killing it. The deal is complex — a specific issue of shares to Titan in exchange for them acquiring the Wiese-related parties' ordinary shares and cancelling the deferred shares — but the outcome is simple: one share, one vote.

The Independent Board, chaired by Wendy Lucas-Bull, has blessed the transaction and recommends shareholders vote in favour. The circular goes out on 10 December and the general meeting is set for 19 January 2027. At R335.81, Shoprite is already the best-run retailer in Africa, and removing this governance overhang only makes it more attractive to institutional investors who have been sitting on the sidelines. Bullish because this unlocks the next leg of re-rating.

Trading statement — EPS +60-70% but HEPS down 88-98% on sugar imports
← Previous: NEUTRAL on 23 Sep
EPS up 60-70% to 206-219 cents thanks to an impairment reversal on Mozambican macadamia operations. Headline earnings, which excludes that reversal, collapsed 88-98% as cheap sugar imports hammered the core business.

This is a study in why headline earnings matter. Crookes Brothers reported EPS up 60-70%, which sounds fantastic until you read that the increase is driven entirely by reversing a prior impairment on macadamia operations that are now classified as held for sale. Headline earnings — the number that measures the actual trading performance — crashed by 88-98% because cheap sugar imports from the global market are destroying the SACU sugar price. Industrial action at South African mills made it worse, delaying deliveries and pushing revenue into the second half.

The banana segment did well, but bananas are not big enough to move the needle. At R23.50 the stock looks optically cheap on EPS, but the headline earnings story is ugly and there is no quick fix for the sugar import problem. Neutral because the impairment reversal is a one-off that flatters the numbers, and the underlying business is under genuine pressure.
