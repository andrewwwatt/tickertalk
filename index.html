Novus Holdings Limited (NVS)
Trading statement H1 2027 — EPS more than doubles on Mustek consolidation
← Previous: NEUTRAL on 05 Oct
EPS expected between 208.03 and 210.69 cents, more than 100% higher than the prior period, driven by the consolidation of Mustek as a subsidiary from June 2026.

Novus Holdings put out a trading statement that is almost entirely about Mustek. The printing and packaging group took control of Mustek on 30 June 2026, and the numbers for the six months to September show exactly why they did it. EPS is heading for 208 to 210 cents, more than double the prior corresponding period. That is the Mustek consolidation effect — the ICT segment is now a subsidiary rather than an associate, and every cent of its earnings flows through to Novus's bottom line. The acquisition has transformed the earnings base overnight.

The question is whether the transformation is durable. Printing is a declining industry in South Africa, and Novus's core business has been a slow bleed for years. Mustek gives it a second act in ICT distribution, but the integration risks are real. At R5.92 the stock trades on a trailing PE of roughly 3 — that is absurdly cheap for a business earning 208 cents per share, but the discount reflects the market's uncertainty about whether the Mustek earnings are sustainable. If they can execute, this is a multi-bagger. If they cannot, the share price already prices in the worst case.

BULLISHReview #5
NEPI Rockcastle N.V. (NRP)
EUR 500 million green bond priced — 4.75% coupon, 5x oversubscribed
← Previous: NEUTRAL on 17 Nov
NEPI Rockcastle priced a EUR 500 million senior unsecured 5-year green bond at 4.75% coupon, rated BBB+/BBB+. The orderbook peaked at EUR 2.7 billion — more than 5x oversubscribed.

NEPI Rockcastle went to the European bond market and got a standing ovation. The EUR 500 million green bond — a 5-year senior unsecured note at 4.75% — drew an orderbook of EUR 2.7 billion from 110 investors, more than five times the issue size. That is not just a successful fundraise; it is a statement about the quality of NEPI's retail portfolio and the market's appetite for well-structured property credit in Central and Eastern Europe. The bond is rated BBB+ by both S&P and Fitch, and 89% of the allocation went to asset managers — UK investors took 41% of the deal.

What matters for shareholders is the cost and availability of capital. NEPI locked in 4.75% for five years in a market where sovereign borrowing costs are rising. The bond extends the group's average debt maturity and increases the share of sustainable debt in its funding mix. At R144.39 the stock yields roughly 7-8% and trades at a discount to net asset value, which is standard for SA property counters. But the refinancing risk that haunts smaller property stocks is simply not a concern for NEPI. This is a well-run machine accessing cheap capital in a world where cheap capital is becoming scarce.

Nampak Limited (NPK)
Trading statement FY2026 — normalised HEPS up 2-8% but headline earnings crumble
Normalised HEPS managed 2-8% growth to 7,500-7,900 cents, but HEPS crashed 34-38% to 6,500-6,900 cents and EPS fell 26-31% to 9,700-10,300 cents. Nampak also announced a sell-down of its NZL stake from 51.43% to 19.99% for $8 million.

Nampak's trading statement requires a decoder ring. Normalised headline earnings per share — the number management wants you to look at — grew 2% to 8%, landing at 7,500 to 7,900 cents. That is modest but positive, and the R146 million post-tax reduction in finance costs suggests the deleveraging story is real, even if it is happening slowly. But strip away the normalisation and the picture gets ugly. Headline EPS fell 34% to 38%, and basic EPS dropped 26% to 31%. The prior year had R47 million in pension surplus and R195 million in Covid insurance claims that did not repeat, plus R91 million in Angolan relocation costs this year.

The bigger news buried in the statement is the planned sell-down of Nampak Zimbabwe from 51.43% to 19.99% for $8 million total consideration. That is a fire sale price for a business that has been a perpetual headache, and it signals that Nampak's management has finally accepted that Zimbabwe is not coming back. The stock trades at R502.50 — roughly 6.5-7 times normalised earnings — which looks cheap until you remember that normalised earnings exclude half the things that keep going wrong. This is a value trap until the Diversified division stabilises and the Zimbabwe overhang is fully gone.

BULLISHReview #1
Mr Price Group Limited (MRP)
Interim results — revenue +40.5%, Africa margins expand, NKD delivers
Group revenue surged 40.5% to R26.1bn on the NKD acquisition, but the Africa business grew retail sales 3.1% ahead of the market, expanded gross margin 60bps to 40.6%, and grew operating profit 5.9%. Interim dividend of 329.5 cents per share.

Mr Price's interim results are two stories in one. The headline number — revenue up 40.5% to R26.1 billion — is all NKD, the German value retailer acquired in March. NKD contributed R6.9 billion in sales and R526 million in operating profit, and management says it outperformed both the German total and value apparel markets. That is a decent start for a European adventure that could have gone either way. But the real story is in Africa, where retail sales grew 3.1% against a market doing 1.2%, gross margins expanded 60 basis points to 40.6%, and operating margins hit 11.8%. In a consumer environment described as 'persistent volatility and heightened unpredictability', Mr Price is taking share, protecting margins, and generating cash. Cash sales accounted for 91.4% of group sales, which is a staggering number for any retailer.

The interim dividend of 329.5 cents per share (63% payout) is a statement of confidence. The balance sheet is solid, cash generation is strong at R4.8 billion from operations, and 145 new stores were opened in the period. The only real question is whether NKD can sustain its momentum through a German winter in a cost-of-living crisis. On the Africa side, there is nothing to fault: Mr Price is doing exactly what a value retailer should do in a constrained economy — giving customers a reason to trade down and keeping them there. At R178.99, the stock trades at roughly 14-15 times annualised earnings for a business compounding at double-digits. That is not cheap enough to be a steal, but it is fair value for a retailer that keeps winning.

BULLISHReview #1
Deneb Investments Limited (DNB)
Category 1 disposal — Formex sold for R435m enterprise value
Deneb is selling its wholly owned automotive component manufacturer Formex Industries to VM Automotive for an enterprise value of R435 million. A R40 million loan claim is retained and will be repaid within 30 days of closing.

Deneb is selling Formex, its automotive components subsidiary, to VM Automotive for an enterprise value of R435 million. This is a Category 1 disposal — the largest transaction a company can do without shareholder approval being automatic. The purchase consideration is based on an agreed enterprise value of R435 million on a cash-free, debt-free basis, and critically, Deneb is retaining a R40 million loan claim that Formex must repay within 30 days of closing. That R40 million is effectively a cash bonus that arrives shortly after the deal closes.

The logic of the disposal is straightforward: Deneb is exiting a capital-intensive manufacturing business in a sector facing structural headwinds. At R2.85, Deneb's market cap is roughly R300 million, meaning the Formex sale alone justifies most of the company's current valuation. The remaining portfolio — property and other investments — is essentially free. That is a compelling setup for shareholders who trust management to deploy the cash wisely. The risk is that they do not, but the structure of this deal suggests discipline, not desperation.

Copper 360 Limited (CPR)
Trading statement H1 2027 — losses shrink 80-85% but still in the red
← Previous: BULLISH on 16 Nov
Loss per share improved from 18.74 cents to 2.81-3.75 cents, an 80-85% reduction. The company is still loss-making but the trajectory is clearly improving as copper production ramps up.

Copper 360 is still losing money, but the losses are shrinking fast. For the six months to August, the basic loss per share improved from 18.74 cents to somewhere between 2.81 and 3.75 cents — an 80% to 85% improvement. That is a genuine operational turnaround, driven by higher copper production and better cost control at the Northern Cape operations. The company is not yet profitable, but it is close enough that the next quarter or two could tip it over the line.

The stock trades at 61 cents, which is essentially a call option on copper prices and operational execution. The balance sheet remains the biggest risk — junior miners burn cash, and Copper 360 has limited room for error. If copper holds above $4.00/lb and the ramp-up continues on schedule, this stock could rerate quickly. If either falters, the losses could widen again just as fast. Neutral for now, but this is one to watch: the direction of travel is finally right.
