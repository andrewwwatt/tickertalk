Spear REIT (SEA)
Unaudited financial results and dividend declaration for the six months ended 31 August 2026
← Previous: BULLISH on 27 Aug
Spear grew its distribution 8% to 44.92c with vacancy of just 1.1% and LTV trimmed to 21.9% — the Western Cape specialist keeps compounding quietly while the rest of property whines about vacancies.

Distributable income per share rose 8% to 47.29c, the payout is held at 95% and the distribution came in at 44.92c. Revenue including smoothing jumped 28.6% to R508.7m, the portfolio revalued up to R8.8bn across 44 properties, and reversions turned positive at +7.98% all-property (from -2.28% last year). Interest cover is a healthy 6.31x and LTV dropped to 21.9%. In a property market where vacancy is the story, Spear's 1.11% total vacancy is barely a rounding error.\n\nThe strategy is the concentration: all Western Cape, nothing else, deliberately. No other listed REIT commits so hard to one province, and the region keeps delivering — reliable infrastructure, a resilient economy, and private money chasing it. TNAV is up to R13.33 a share against a R13.10 price, so you're buying roughly at book with an annualised yield around 7% and 8% distribution growth. That's a boring, beautiful property stock. BULLISH.

Further Cautionary Announcement
ArcelorMittal SA is still under cautionary — no binding offer, ministerial approval outstanding — yet the market pushed the shares up 6.7% on deal hopes. That's speculation with the company's own 'no certainty' warning attached.

The facts are thin by design: ArcelorMittal SA, ArcelorMittal Group and the IDC remain at an advanced stage of talks on a potential transaction, but pre-conditions — including ministerial approval — are outstanding and no binding offer is in place. The board's language is deliberately cold: there is no certainty a transaction will be concluded. This is the second cautionary since 12 October, and the company is still not in a position to share details of what a deal might look like.\n\nThe market doesn't care: the share rose from R1.06 to R1.27, adding 6.7% on press speculation about a steel-industry rescue. But read the cautionary carefully — the company itself is telling you to be careful, its balance sheet is deeply impaired (negative book value), and the IDC's involvement suggests a rescue, not a windfall. When the announcement says 'no certainty' and the share jumps nearly 7%, that's hope being priced, not earnings. NEUTRAL — own the caution, don't buy the rumour.
