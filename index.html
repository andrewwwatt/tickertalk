Hyprop Investments (HYP)
Consolidated Audited Results for the Year Ended 30 June 2026, Cash Dividend and Outlook
Hyprop delivered a double-digit rise in distributable income, hitting the upper end of guidance. The shopping centre REIT is benefiting from strong tenant demand, well-located malls, and a recovering consumer. The dividend was lifted accordingly. Price rose 1.4% on results day, the market's way of saying 'good job.' Current R58.79. A rare bright spot in SA retail property.

Hyprop just delivered the kind of set that makes you wonder why SA property gets so little love. Distributable income came in at the upper end of guidance, driven by strong trading from the core South African mall portfolio. Canal Walk, Clearwater, and Hyde Park are drawing foot traffic and tenant demand that contradicts the narrative of a dying retail sector. Vacancies are low, rental reversions are improving, and cost discipline is evident in the margin.

The international portfolio (Eastern Europe) remains a drag — weaker currencies and geopolitical uncertainty — but it's not derailing the overall story. The balance sheet is conservative with moderate LTV and good interest cover. Management declared a higher dividend, signalling confidence. At R58.79, Hyprop trades on about a 9% forward yield with distributable income growing double-digits. In a world where SA government bonds yield ~10.5%, this equity risk premium is too narrow for the quality of the portfolio. The stock rose 1.4% on results day, and we think there's more to come as the consumer recovery gains traction. A quality mall REIT doing what it should — executing. Rating: BULLISH.

Growthpoint Properties (GRT)
Group Audited Annual Results and Cash Dividend Declaration for the Year Ended 30 June 2026
Growthpoint delivered a steady set of FY2026 numbers but the market wanted more. Distributable income per share grew modestly. The V&A Waterfront continued its stellar run as tourism and retail demand surged. But Gauteng office vacancies remain painful — R4.9bn in assets were sold to pivot out of empty offices. CEO Norbert Sasse is retiring after 26 years, marking the end of an era. Price fell 3.1% on results day.

This is a tale of two property markets living inside the same REIT. The V&A Waterfront is firing on all cylinders — international tourism recovery, strong retail demand, premium office occupancy. That asset alone justifies a material chunk of Growthpoint's R55bn portfolio valuation. But Gauteng offices are a different story. Vacancies are structural — companies aren't coming back to Sandton towers five days a week. Management sold R4.9bn in assets over the year to accelerate the pivot toward better-performing sectors and geographies. That's the right strategy, but it takes time to flow through to distributable income per share.

The bigger story is the CEO transition. Norbert Sasse is stepping down after 26 years — he built Growthpoint into the largest SA REIT. His successor inherits a portfolio in transition, a balance sheet that's manageable (interest cover around 2.5x, LTV in the mid-30s), and a market that trades the stock at ~11x PE with a ~7.5% yield. At R15.94, the stock is down 10% from its August high. The 3.1% drop on results day suggests the market wanted faster progress on the portfolio pivot. We'll wait for the new CEO's strategy before getting more constructive.
