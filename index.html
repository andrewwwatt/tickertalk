NEUTRALReview #2
Zeda Limited (ZZD)
Annual Results FY2026 — HEPS -18.1%, revenue flat, dividend 80c maintained
← Previous: BULLISH on 17 Nov
HEPS fell 18.1% to 295.5c on flat revenue of R10.7bn. Operating margin compressed from 15.2% to 13.3%. Net debt rose to R5.7bn. ROIC of 11.4% still beats WACC of 10.5% by 90bps. Dividend declared at 80c per share.

Zeda's FY2026 numbers are a study in headwinds. Revenue was flat at R10.7 billion, EBITDA flat at R3.1 billion, but headline earnings per share dropped 18.1% to 295.5 cents because depreciation ate into the margin — operating margin fell from 15.2% to 13.3%. The company blames new-vehicle deflation, Chinese OEM competition narrowing the price gap with one-year-old cars, and rising interest rates squeezing consumer affordability. These are real problems, and they are not going away next quarter. The board declared an 80 cent dividend (roughly 2.6% yield at R15.15), which is a show of confidence, but earnings are heading in the wrong direction.

The one genuinely positive data point is ROIC of 11.4% against a WACC of 10.5% — a 90 basis point spread that proves Zeda is still creating value from its capital-intensive model, just less of it. Net debt rose to R5.7 billion (1.9x EBITDA), which is manageable but moving the wrong way. The stock was up 2% on the day, which feels like the market giving management credit for holding the line rather than celebrating the result. Zeda is not in crisis, but it is in a cyclical trough, and the dividend alone is not enough to make this a compelling entry at current levels.

Section 122 notice — Ninety One SA disposed below 5% threshold
← Previous: NEUTRAL on 23 Oct
Ninety One SA Proprietary Limited disposed of Valterra shares and now holds 4.97% beneficial interest — a routine crossing of the 5% reporting threshold. No change to company fundamentals.

Valterra Platinum filed a Section 122 notice because Ninety One SA trimmed its stake below the 5% threshold to 4.97%. This is a regulatory disclosure, not a trading signal. Fund managers reduce positions below 5% all the time — it simplifies their reporting obligations and may have nothing to do with their view on the business. The stock at R1,094 is down 3.5% on the day, which could be the market reading too much into an administrative filing.

Valterra remains a high-risk platinum miner with a dual JSE/LSE listing, and the fundamentals have not changed because one asset manager shuffled its books. The Section 122 notice tells you nothing about production, costs, or platinum group metal prices — all of which matter far more than a 3-basis-point change in a fund manager's declared holding. Ignore the noise.

Interim results H1 FY2027 — HEPS +7%, income flattish, net debt down 12%
Total income R5.4bn (-2%), adjusted EBITDA R1.6bn (-4%), HEPS 79c (+7%), interim dividend flat at 15c. Net debt reduced 12% to R5.99bn (1.81x EBITDA). Price R7.04, +2% on the day.

Tsogo Sun put out interim numbers that read like a holding pattern with a decent tailwind. Total income of R5.43 billion was 2% lower, operating costs came down by the same amount, and adjusted EBITDA slipped 4% to R1.65 billion. The headline saving grace is headline earnings per share: 79 cents against 74 cents last year, a 7% improvement that the market rewarded with a 2% bump in the share price to R7.04. That divergence — income down, HEPS up — tells you the company is squeezing efficiency out of a broadly flat revenue base. Net debt dropped 12% to R5.99 billion, bringing the leverage ratio to a comfortable 1.81x EBITDA from 2.01x.\n\nThe dividend is flat at 15 cents per share (roughly 2.1% yield), which is the board saying 'we can afford this but we are not convinced the trajectory is strong enough to increase it.' That is fair. Tsogo Sun's hotels and casinos are doing okay — occupancy is steady, gaming revenue is resilient, and the balance sheet is in its best shape since the Casino Act — but 'okay' does not get a BULLISH rating. The stock is not expensive at roughly 9 times annualised HEPS, and the debt reduction is genuine progress, but the business is not growing meaningfully and the dividend yield alone does not make it a screaming buy. NEUTRAL because it is fine, well-run and boring — and sometimes that is exactly what you want in a casino stock.

Prosus delivered the top line everyone wanted and the bottom line nobody likes — and the bottom line is the part that matters less than it looks. The answer is that the discount — currently around 35% — has narrowed from the 45%+ lows but remains stubbornly wide for a holding company that is demonstrably executing. The investment in JET and iFood is the right strategic bet: build ecosystems that work without Tencent's profits propping them up. The market sold Prosus off 0.5% on the day, which feels like a shrug at a set of numbers that deserve a nod. At R597, the stock offers meaningful upside when the market remembers that a 12% earnings growth rate in a portfolio worth US$140 billion is worth paying for.

Pepkor Holdings Limited (PPH)
Reviewed annual results FY2026 — HEPS +6.9%, revenue tops R100bn, dividend +9.3%
← Previous: NEUTRAL on 30 Oct
Revenue smashed through R100bn for the first time (R105.9bn, +11.9%), HEPS rose 6.9% to 171.3c (normalised +9.7%), gross margin expanded 170bps to 41.5%, and free cash flow more than doubled to R4.8bn. Dividend up 9.3% to 57.9c. Yet the stock fell 5.18% on the day — the market wanted more.

Pepkor just delivered a set of results that would make most SA retailers envious. Revenue crossed R100 billion for the first time at R105.9 billion — up nearly 12% — and the gross profit margin widened 170 basis points to 41.5%, which in retail is the difference between a good year and a great one. Normalised HEPS rose 9.7% to 177.5 cents, the dividend went up 9.3% to 57.9 cents, and free cash flow more than doubled to R4.8 billion. The PlusB banking investment is still a drag on reported numbers, but the core retail machine is firing on all cylinders — organic revenue growth of 7.3% on a base this size is no accident.

So why did the stock drop 5% on the day? Because the market priced in this result weeks ago and was hoping for a blowout instead of a solid beat. At R17.22 the stock trades on roughly 10 times normalised earnings with a 3.4% yield, which is cheap for a retailer with 41.5% gross margins, double-digit cash conversion and a 12.5% two-year normalised HEPS CAGR. The sell-off is the market being greedy; the business is doing exactly what it should. Buy the dip, collect the dividend, ignore the noise.

Nutun Limited (NTU)
Trading statement FY2026 — Loss narrowing 41-47% but core loss widening 34-48%
Headline loss per share -7.7c to -8.5c (improvement from -14.4c), but core loss (non-IFRS measure stripping out once-offs) widened 34-48% to -7.7c to -8.5c from -5.7c. Rand strength and elevated amortisation continue to weigh. Price R0.94.

Nutun (formerly Transaction Capital) reported its first full financial year as a simplified group, and the numbers show a business that has stopped bleeding but has not yet learned to walk. The headline loss per share narrowed from -14.4 cents to between -7.7 and -8.5 cents — a 41-47% improvement that management will frame as progress, and technically it is. The operating story has two clear headwinds: an extended period of rand strength that crushed Nutun International's rand-denominated earnings, and elevated amortisation of the legacy book debt portfolios that is a function of the wind-down, not the underlying collection performance. Both are real problems. Neither is going away in Q1.\n\nThe genuinely worrying number is the core loss, which management's own non-IFRS measure shows WIDENED by 34-48% to between -7.7 and -8.5 cents from -5.7 cents. That means the adjusted, once-off-stripped, underlying business is actually losing MORE money than before, not less. The share price of R0.94 — down 1% on the day — values the company at roughly 12 times the midpoint of the headline loss, which is not a valuation, it is a hope. Nutun needs the rand to weaken and the consumer to recover before it can turn these narrow losses into even narrow profits. The direction of travel is positive, but the distance to breakeven is still measured in years, not quarters. BEARISH because a business whose core losses are widening is not cheap, it is just bad.

LPS and HLPS expected between 0.37c and 8.95c, an improvement of 97-99% from 285.98c in H1 FY2026. The prior year's massive loss was a once-off IFRS 2 share-based listing expense of R217m from a reverse takeover. Underlying business still uncertain. Price R0.90.

Cilo Cybin reported a trading statement that shows a 97% to 99% improvement in both loss per share and headline loss per share, from a staggering -285.98 cents to between -0.37 and -8.95 cents. That looks like a miracle turnaround until you read the small print: the prior year included a once-off IFRS 2 share-based listing expense of R217 million because the company's 2025 reverse takeover of Cilo Cybin Pharmaceutical did not qualify as a 'business' under IFRS 3, and the entire premium paid to get the listing was expensed through the P&L. Strip that out, and the improvement is less dramatic — the underlying pharmaceutical business is still in its early commercial stage.\n\nThe stock trades at R0.90 with a market cap of roughly R170 million, which is pricing in that Cilo Cybin will eventually generate revenue from its pharmaceutical products without another accounting landmine blowing up the P&L. The IFRS 2 charge is gone, the structure is clean, and the company is now a legitimate operating business instead of a listing shell. That is the bull case. The bear case is that a company losing between 0.4 and 9 cents per share in its 'improved' state is still losing money, has no clear path to profitability disclosed in this statement, and trades on thin liquidity. NEUTRAL because the accounting clean-up is real progress but the underlying business is still unproven, and optimism priced at R0.90 is cheap for a reason.
