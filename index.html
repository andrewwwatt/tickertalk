Southern Sun Limited (SSU)
Interim results for six months ended 30 September 2026 — EPS +31-39%
← Previous: NEUTRAL on 15 Sep
Occupancy rose to 61.8%, room rates up 9%, revenue grew 12% and finance costs dropped 67% after dollar debt was repaid. EPS of 32-34 cents, up 31-39%.

Southern Sun put out a set of interim numbers that tell a simple story: more people are staying in its hotels, they are paying more per room, and the company is spending less on debt. Occupancy went from 59.1% to 61.8%, average room rates rose 9%, total revenue grew 12% (10% in SA, 55% offshore — the offshore number is off a tiny base but the direction matters). Operating expense growth was contained to 8% despite IT, utilities, property rates and OTAs all taking their cut. Net finance costs fell 67% because the dollar-denominated debt is gone and cash generation is doing the rest.\n\nEPS is heading for 32-34 cents against 24.5 cents last year — a 31-39% improvement — and headline earnings are up 35-44%. The stock sits at R10.87, which is roughly 16 times annualised earnings, and the balance sheet is in its best shape in years. The risk is that occupancy above 62% in a seasonally weaker half implies a strong second half, and the market will price that expectation in now. But the trajectory is unambiguous, and the full results land on 18 November. This is a hotel group doing what a well-run hotel group should: filling beds, raising prices and paying down debt.

Appointment of Chairman and Independent Non-Executive Director
← Previous: NEUTRAL on 05 Oct
Lwazi Koyana, acting chairman since August, appointed as permanent chairman. Grant Pattison appointed as INED from February 2027. Both bring deep retail and FMCG experience.

Spar formalised its board leadership: Lwazi Koyana, who has been acting chairman since August, gets the permanent chair with effect from 12 November. He is a chartered accountant with 30 years in investment management and corporate finance, and as the longest-serving director on the board, he provides the continuity Spar needs after a turbulent few years. Separately, Grant Pattison — an electrical engineer with serious retail and FMCG credentials — has accepted an offer to join as independent non-executive director from 25 February 2027. The interesting detail is Pattison: his background in retail turnaround and operational management is precisely the skillset a wholesaler under margin pressure needs on its board. If Roux also accepts, Spar gets two experienced non-executives in one go. But governance changes take years to show up in the P&L, and NEUTRAL is the right call for an announcement that changes who sits in the chairs, not what the business earns.

BULLISHReview #1
Life Healthcare Group Holdings (LHC)
Trading statement for the year ended 30 September 2026 — NEPS +6-11%
Normalised EPS up 6-11% to 106-111 cents, but headline numbers are a mess after the R2.4bn LMI disposal gain in FY2025. The underlying business in southern Africa is slowly, quietly getting better.

Life Healthcare reported its FY2026 trading statement and the headline numbers are worse than useless — EPS went from -106.9c to +94-99c, which sounds miraculous until you read that the prior year included a R2.4 billion gain from selling LMI and this year does not. The disposal created a fair value adjustment of R2.9 billion that flew through the P&L in FY2025 and vanished in FY2026, making every per-share metric except NEPS a comparison of apples to nuclear submarines.\n\nNormalised earnings per share — the number management uses to run the business — came in at 106-111 cents against 100 cents last year, a 6% to 11% improvement. That is not spectacular for a hospital group with pricing power and a defensive patient base, but it is real, and it is the only number in this statement that measures anything. At R12.45 the market is paying 11-12 times normalised earnings for a business that owns the second-largest private hospital network in South Africa, has paid down dollar debt, and is not reliant on the LMI sale to fund its dividend. Cheap, boring and honest. The numbers that matter are the ones that strip out the noise, and those point higher.

BEARISHReview #1
Kibo Energy PLC (KBO)
Shareholder Update and Resignation of Director — still suspended, CEO out
Kibo remains suspended since August 2025 for non-publication of FY2024 and FY2025 financials. CEO Cobus van der Merwe resigned. Company is in "active negotiations" for an energy project acquisition but nothing is binding.

Kibo has been suspended since August 2025, has not published its 2024 or 2025 annual financial statements, lost its AIM listing in July 2026, and now its CEO Cobus van der Merwe has resigned effective 31 October. The company says it is in 'active negotiations' for an energy-related project in South Africa with associated funding, and hopes the transaction — together with the appointment of auditors and publication of outstanding financials — will form the basis of an application to lift the suspension. The word 'hopes' is doing heavy lifting in that sentence.\n\nNoel O'Keeffe has taken over as interim managing director, and the board is assessing 'alternative transactions' to give Kibo a new business and funding. This is the language of a shell company that is running out of time. The JSE recognises Kibo's listing as a primary listing now that AIM is gone, and the compliance requirements are steeper. A stock trading at 1 cent with no financials, no CEO and no binding transaction is not an investment thesis — it is a call option on a miracle. BEARISH because the most likely outcome is that the suspension becomes permanent.

BULLISHReview #2
Goldrush Holdings (GDH)
Trading statement for interim period ended 30 September 2026 — EPS +551-571%
← Previous: BEARISH on 04 Nov
EPS of 41.95-43.30 cents against 6.45 cents — a 551-571% surge. HEPS of 35-36.4 cents, up 391-411%. The difference reflects a profit on disposal of non-current assets.

Goldrush delivered a trading statement that makes you double-check the decimal points. EPS of 41.95 to 43.30 cents against 6.45 cents in the prior period — a 551% to 571% increase — and headline earnings per share of 35 to 36.4 cents, up 391% to 411%. The gap between EPS and HEPS is a profit on disposal of non-current assets, so not all of this growth came from operations, but the headline number is still a five-fold improvement off a very low base. The full interim results land on 16 November and the market will be looking for signs that the earnings trajectory is sustainable, not just a one-off clean-up.\n\nThe stock is not listed on Yahoo Finance and trades infrequently, which makes this a punt on an illiquid small cap rather than a trade you can execute at a fair price. But the numbers in this statement are unambiguous: Goldrush is generating real earnings growth from a base so low that even modest operational improvement produces triple-digit percentage changes. The risk is that 'growth off a low base' is not the same as 'good business,' and at 79 cents (last traded price on the JSE) you are buying a lottery ticket on a company that just proved it can make money — not that it can keep making it.

← Previous: NEUTRAL on 10 Nov
Headline loss per share of 12.3c (wider than 9.7c loss last year). Net asset value collapsed 44% to 287c per share from 512c. Net cash inflow from operations is positive at R253m but the earnings picture is getting worse.

enX reported a full-year loss of 12.3 cents per share from continuing operations, wider than the 9.7 cent loss last year, and net asset value per share fell 44% to 287 cents from 512 cents — a R2.85 stock is now trading at a 20% discount to a rapidly shrinking book value, which sounds like a value trap and reads like one. The loss before tax was R12 million, roughly flat on the R1 million loss last year when you stop pretending the rounding matters, and total headline loss was 9.9 cents against a profit of 53.2 cents in FY2025 when discontinued operations still contributed.\n\nThe only bright spot is operating cash flow: R253 million net inflow before financing, roughly the same as last year's R241 million, which suggests the underlying industrial equipment and leasing businesses still generate cash even when they cannot turn an accounting profit. But a company whose book value is evaporating at 44% a year, whose continuing operations are loss-making and whose stock trades at R2.35 is not an investment. It is a restructuring story that has not restructured, and the share price is being honest about it.
