NEUTRALReview #2
Zeda Limited (ZZD)
Annual Results FY2026 — HEPS -18.1%, revenue flat, dividend 80c maintained
← Previous: BULLISH on 17 Nov
HEPS fell 18.1% to 295.5c on flat revenue of R10.7bn. Operating margin compressed from 15.2% to 13.3%. Net debt rose to R5.7bn. ROIC of 11.4% still beats WACC of 10.5% by 90bps. Dividend declared at 80c per share.

Zeda's FY2026 numbers are a study in headwinds. Revenue was flat at R10.7 billion, EBITDA flat at R3.1 billion, but headline earnings per share dropped 18.1% to 295.5 cents because depreciation ate into the margin — operating margin fell from 15.2% to 13.3%. The company blames new-vehicle deflation, Chinese OEM competition narrowing the price gap with one-year-old cars, and rising interest rates squeezing consumer affordability. These are real problems, and they are not going away next quarter. The board declared an 80 cent dividend (roughly 2.6% yield at R15.15), which is a show of confidence, but earnings are heading in the wrong direction.

The one genuinely positive data point is ROIC of 11.4% against a WACC of 10.5% — a 90 basis point spread that proves Zeda is still creating value from its capital-intensive model, just less of it. Net debt rose to R5.7 billion (1.9x EBITDA), which is manageable but moving the wrong way. The stock was up 2% on the day, which feels like the market giving management credit for holding the line rather than celebrating the result. Zeda is not in crisis, but it is in a cyclical trough, and the dividend alone is not enough to make this a compelling entry at current levels.

Valterra Platinum Limited (VAL)
Section 122 notice — Ninety One SA disposed below 5% threshold
← Previous: NEUTRAL on 23 Oct
Ninety One SA Proprietary Limited disposed of Valterra shares and now holds 4.97% beneficial interest — a routine crossing of the 5% reporting threshold. No change to company fundamentals.

Valterra Platinum filed a Section 122 notice because Ninety One SA trimmed its stake below the 5% threshold to 4.97%. This is a regulatory disclosure, not a trading signal. Fund managers reduce positions below 5% all the time — it simplifies their reporting obligations and may have nothing to do with their view on the business. The stock at R1,094 is down 3.5% on the day, which could be the market reading too much into an administrative filing.

Valterra remains a high-risk platinum miner with a dual JSE/LSE listing, and the fundamentals have not changed because one asset manager shuffled its books. The Section 122 notice tells you nothing about production, costs, or platinum group metal prices — all of which matter far more than a 3-basis-point change in a fund manager's declared holding. Ignore the noise.

Interim results H1 FY2026 — Revenue +52%, EPS +254%, HEPS +195%

Revenue surged 52% to R5.5bn, operating profit up 18% to R190m, profit more than tripled to R171.5m, EPS 102.54c (+254%), HEPS 102.03c (+195%). The stock jumped 14% on the day.

Stefanutti Stocks just put out a set of interim numbers that make you wonder if the construction sector has finally turned. Revenue jumped 52% to R5.5 billion, operating profit rose 18% to R190 million, and earnings per share more than tripled to 102.54 cents from 28.96 cents — a 254% increase that is hard to argue with. Headline earnings per share of 102.03 cents were up 195%, which means the profit quality is real and not just a one-off asset sale. A construction company making real money and growing it fast is not something the JSE sees every day.

The stock reacted accordingly, up 14% to R6.60, and the market cap is now roughly R1.1 billion against an annualised run-rate of R340 million in profits — a forward PE of about 6.5 times. That is cheap if the construction cycle has legs, and Stefanutti has the order book and the revenue trajectory to suggest it does. The risk is that construction margins are volatile, working capital eats cash, and one bad project can wipe out a quarter's gains. But the numbers on the page today are unambiguous: this is a turnaround that is actually turning.

Pepkor Holdings Limited (PPH)
Reviewed annual results FY2026 — HEPS +6.9%, revenue tops R100bn, dividend +9.3%
← Previous: NEUTRAL on 30 Oct
Revenue smashed through R100bn for the first time (R105.9bn, +11.9%), HEPS rose 6.9% to 171.3c (normalised +9.7%), gross margin expanded 170bps to 41.5%, and free cash flow more than doubled to R4.8bn. Dividend up 9.3% to 57.9c. Yet the stock fell 5.18% on the day — the market wanted more.

Pepkor just delivered a set of results that would make most SA retailers envious. Revenue crossed R100 billion for the first time at R105.9 billion — up nearly 12% — and the gross profit margin widened 170 basis points to 41.5%, which in retail is the difference between a good year and a great one. Normalised HEPS rose 9.7% to 177.5 cents, the dividend went up 9.3% to 57.9 cents, and free cash flow more than doubled to R4.8 billion. The PlusB banking investment is still a drag on reported numbers, but the core retail machine is firing on all cylinders — organic revenue growth of 7.3% on a base this size is no accident.

So why did the stock drop 5% on the day? Because the market priced in this result weeks ago and was hoping for a blowout instead of a solid beat. At R17.22 the stock trades on roughly 10 times normalised earnings with a 3.4% yield, which is cheap for a retailer with 41.5% gross margins, double-digit cash conversion and a 12.5% two-year normalised HEPS CAGR. The sell-off is the market being greedy; the business is doing exactly what it should. Buy the dip, collect the dividend, ignore the noise.
